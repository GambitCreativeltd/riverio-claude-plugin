# Writing the new beats

The canvas's agents turn the Prompt text into a script/story, character images and 5 clip prompts (10 s each,
one ~50 s ad). Your story IS that Prompt text, so copy the SOURCE beat's format exactly: same headings, same
speaker labels, same length, same kind of ending. Only the situation, the people and the lines change.

## What makes a beat work
- **Same product truth every time.** Read the source story's NOTES: what the product is (e.g. a comparison site,
  not an insurer), which claims are hedged ("see how much you could lower..."), which numbers are spoken only.
  Keep a number the brand segment shows (e.g. the price that the price card counts down from) so the reused
  brand pieces still fit.
- **About 25 spoken words per 10 s clip.** More gets rushed or cut.
- **Each line is said by ONE character; the other stays silent.** Put it in the story ("the other stays silent
  while she speaks"). Short interjections ("Says who?") are where video models mix up voices - use few, or none.
- **Two characters, very different looks.** Different ages/genders, and clearly different outfits and colours
  (e.g. mustard hoodie vs red tee). Look-alikes get swapped mid-scene.
- **The call to action says the brand name in full** and invites: "Go to <Brand Name> and see how much you could...".
  Never a sentence where the brand could be heard as the thing to avoid ("Don't settle for <Brand Name>").
- **No readable logos, names or prices on screens, letters or signs** in the story - video models garble text.
  Say "quotes line up side by side" instead of describing a screen.
- **Soften words that trip safety filters** (dating, freezing in fear, violence, kids in danger).
- Comic and relatable, never mean. Frustration is aimed at the situation, never at a person or a real company.

## Podcast style (two people at a desk)
Keep the Prompt node to the brief + the one-line DIALOGUE FORMAT rule the source uses. Every rule you put in the
Prompt is read by ALL the agents: a "video prompt rule" there made the script agent write monologues and the
cast agent draw storyboards. Keep hair/wardrobe markers for both speakers in the brief so speakers don't blend.
Headlines of static ad ideas make good hooks for podcast beats.

## Story style (acted / cartoon)
`BEAT 1:` .. `BEAT 5:` with a scene line, then `NAME: line` / `NAME: (action) line`. Keep a "signature moment"
(one visual gag) and protect it in the notes. Beat 4 is where the product shows up; beat 5 ends on the CTA.
Clip 4's lead-in is cut around the brand segment, so its first second and its last line matter most.

## Interview style (street interview)
One person on camera, an interviewer heard but not seen. `CLIP k` blocks: `INTERVIEWER (off-camera): "..."` then
`<PERSON> (on-camera): "..." (delivery note)`. Every clip is the same person and place; no phone screens or on-screen
text in the clips (an app demo, if the source ad has one, is added in the edit). The last clip ends on the brand name
in full and the hedged invite. Keep the source's number of clips exactly (count its video nodes).

## Show them together
Show every beat's story in one message (title + the lines), ask for "yes" or edits, and don't clone before a yes.
